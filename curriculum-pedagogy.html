💛 Philosophy of Teaching & Learning

Teaching and learning is a kaleidoscope of possibilities

My philosophy of teaching and learning, developed for ECE 245 using the Reggio Emilia curriculum model. Each facet of the kaleidoscope is one part of a child’s learning, and every turn creates a new pattern.

The child · pieces The family · mirrors The educator · lens The community · pattern The environment · light
Reggio Emiliathe turn
“

I believe learning is never made from one piece alone. When children, families, educators, community and environment come together through relationships, reflection and inquiry, new patterns of possibility emerge, and no two patterns are ever the same.

My commitment

I will not teach children to fit a fixed pattern. I will listen, observe, reflect and turn the kaleidoscope with them, so new possibilities can emerge.

Facet 1 · The child

Coloured pieces

Children are like the coloured pieces of a kaleidoscope: each one brings a colour, shape and story no other piece can.

In practice: if children became curious about shadows, I would offer flashlights, coloured acetate and translucent loose parts, write down their theories in their own words, and invite them to test and revise those ideas over several days.

Malaguzzi (1994)

Facet 2 · The family

Mirrors

Families are the mirrors: they reflect each child’s identity back into learning and multiply what I can see.

In practice: I would ask families about routines, home languages and celebrations, then bring that knowledge into provocations such as family recipes, songs or labels in home languages. I would also invite families to add their own interpretations to documentation panels.

Ontario Ministry of Education (2014)

Facet 3 · The educator

The lens

The educator is the lens: I do not choose the pattern, I help bring it into focus.

In practice: when a planned experience does not engage children, I would not call it a failure. I would revisit my notes and photos with colleagues, ask what children were communicating, then change the materials, space or timing and try again.

College of Early Childhood Educators (2020)

Facet 4 · The community

The larger pattern

Community is the larger pattern: it connects each piece to people, places and knowledge beyond the classroom.

In practice: I would extend inquiry through community walks and library or garden visits and, following proper protocol, invite an Elder to share stories. I would also ask whose voices are missing from our books and materials.

Toulouse (2018)

Facet 5 · The environment

The light

The environment is the light: without it the pieces stay hidden; with it, endless patterns appear.

In practice: flexible spaces with natural materials, loose parts, mirrors and a light table at children’s height; long blocks of uninterrupted play; and children’s documentation displayed at their eye level.

Wien (2018)

Model · Reggio Emilia

The turn

Every shared turn by children and adults creates a new pattern.

In practice: across every facet, I commit to observing before planning, offering many languages for expression, documenting and revisiting children’s ideas, and planning projects with children, families and community.

Edwards, Gandini & Forman (2012)

🧩 Pedagogical Strategies

Approaches I use in practice

Drawn from my experience in licensed childcare, one-to-one tutoring and classroom teaching.

Play-based, developmentally appropriate learning

I design learning centres and activities that match each child’s developmental stage, using play to build literacy, numeracy, collaboration and self-regulation.

Inquiry-based & student-centred

Questions drive learning. Students investigate, reason and build understanding rather than memorize.

Differentiated & inclusive

I adapt instruction, materials and pace so every learner can take part and make progress.

Foundational literacy

Phonological awareness, phonics, early reading, writing, letter formation and sentence construction.

Foundational numeracy

Number sense and problem-solving, built through hands-on, concrete experiences first.

Positive guidance

Predictable routines, smooth transitions and calm, respectful guidance that help children manage their feelings and behaviour.

📝 Planning & Assessment

How I plan, teach and assess

A continuous cycle that uses formative and summative assessment to guide what comes next.

  1. Observe & assess

    Find out where each learner is through observation and formative assessment.

  2. Plan

    Design developmentally appropriate, Ontario curriculum-aligned experiences and individualized plans.

  3. Teach & adapt

    Deliver engaging, differentiated learning, adjusting in the moment to each child’s needs.

  4. Reflect & share

    Monitor progress, then share goals and next steps with families and colleagues.

📸 Pedagogical Documentation

Colouring with chalk: making learning visible

A group pedagogical documentation for ECE 245, The Reflective (Humber College). We observed preschoolers drawing with chalk outdoors and analysed the experience through How Does Learning Happen? (Ontario Ministry of Education, 2014).

🤝 Belonging 🌱 Well-being 🔍 Engagement 🎨 Expression

What we explored

  1. Which foundations for learning are evident, and how do we know?
  2. What would our next actions be to make the play more responsive and inclusive?
  3. Which stereotypes do the children challenge?
  4. What role does the environment, as the “third teacher”, play?
  5. What questions do we have for the children, and why?

✏️ My contributions

Foundations

“Well-being is evident as the children participate in an outdoor chalk experience where they can independently access the materials and engage in the activity in their own way.”

Next steps

“We could revisit the children’s drawings with them and invite them to explain how their ideas developed while drawing alongside their peers.”

Third teacher

“The outdoor environment acts as a ‘third teacher’ by providing real experiences that become part of the children’s conversations and thinking.”

My question for the children

“Did seeing what the other children were drawing give you any new ideas for your own drawing?”
