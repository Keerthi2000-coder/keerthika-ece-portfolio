📸 A · Pedagogical Documentation

Colouring with Chalk

Our ECE 245 (The Reflective) group pedagogical documentation. We observed preschoolers drawing with chalk outdoors and analysed the experience through How Does Learning Happen? (Ontario Ministry of Education, 2014).

Overview A Pedagogical Documentation B Learning Story C Detailed Responsive Inclusive Plans D Meaningful Artifacts E Resources Used in Practice

Synopsis

“Today the children went outside to explore their creativity using chalk. It was a nice bright sunny breezy day. The children were drawing in the shade side of the concrete using different colours to express their creativity. As they were all engaging in conversation, talking about their creations, they were also asking questions and responding to one other’s questions while exploring their own curiosity.”

  • Group assignment with four classmates, ECE 245, Humber College (2026)
  • Observation: a publicly available preschool video (Harrison, 2014)
  • Children are identified only as Child A to Child E

Foundations for learning we identified

🤝 Belonging 🌱 Well-being 🔍 Engagement 🎨 Expression

My reflective narrative

How this documentation was used in practice, and why I chose it for my portfolio (about 100 to 150 words).

What we explored

  1. Which foundations for learning are evident, and how do we know?
  2. What would our next actions be to make the play more responsive and inclusive?
  3. Which stereotypes do the children challenge?
  4. What role does the environment, as the “third teacher”, play?
  5. What questions do we have for the children, and why?

✏️ My contributions

Foundations

“Well-being is evident as the children participate in an outdoor chalk experience where they can independently access the materials and engage in the activity in their own way.”

Next steps

“We could revisit the children’s drawings with them and invite them to explain how their ideas developed while drawing alongside their peers.”

Third teacher

“The outdoor environment acts as a ‘third teacher’ by providing real experiences that become part of the children’s conversations and thinking.”

My question for the children

“Did seeing what the other children were drawing give you any new ideas for your own drawing?”
